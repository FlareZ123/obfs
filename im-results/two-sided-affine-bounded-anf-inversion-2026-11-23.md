# Deterministic black-box inversion of bounded-degree two-sided affine-wrapped triangular permutations

**23 November 2026 — Agent 37. Intermediate mathematical research, not independently reviewed or release-promoted.** The core deterministic algorithm is [`sims/two-sided-affine-degree-inversion.js`](../sims/two-sided-affine-degree-inversion.js) and its [independent-source differential regression](../test/two-sided-affine-degree-inversion.test.js). Existing [one-sided compatible-permutation theorem](../results/compatible-permutation-one-sided-linear-wrapper-inversion.md) excludes a general two-sided sandwich. Existing [sampled affine flag attack](../sims/affine-flag-oracle-attacker.js) already attacks two-sided examples empirically, with explicit sparse failures. **The distinction here is a deterministic inversion under a declared bounded-ANF-degree premise, including an offline inverse from the already-known full forward interpolation**, not a claim that generic compatible permutations are invertible with polynomially many queries.

## Historical scope: a known vulnerable cryptographic shape

**Important prior art, not a newly discovered vulnerability class.** Goubin and Patarin's *Asymmetric Cryptography with S-Boxes* (ICICS 1997, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/BFb0028492)) studies one-round S-box/triangular structures hidden by two secret affine transformations. Faugère and Perret's *Cryptanalysis of 2R−schemes* (CRYPTO 2006, [public paper](https://www.iacr.org/archive/crypto2006/41170356/41170356.pdf), §3) explicitly describes a quadratic triangular middle map `t ∘ ψ ∘ s` with two secret affine outer layers and states that the one-round triangular constructions were already cryptanalyzed by the original designers; their proposed 2R variants add a **second nonlinear round** and are a different grammar. Our two-sided degree-two source is within that historically known weak *single-round* family. Consequently, **do not claim original discovery that one-round affine-wrapped triangular quadratic maps are invertible or insecure**. The candidate distinct contribution here is a compact exact Boolean-output-affine-coset constructive derivation, its finite query accounting, and independent Node/SCO engineering reproduction. Whether its specific proof and algorithm duplicate a prior published attack has **not** been checked. Promotion as a *novel* academic theorem requires comparing the actual 1997 algorithms, not relying on different names.

## Promise and claim

Let `n` denote the bit width, `D` a known positive *constant*, and `F = A_out ∘ P ∘ A_in : GF(2)^n → GF(2)^n`. Both `A_in` and `A_out` are arbitrary unknown **invertible affine** maps. The middle permutation `P` is triangular/compatible in a hidden bit ordering: each coordinate has the form

`P_i(z) = z_i ⊕ h_i(z_0,...,z_{i-1})`.

Every coordinate's full algebraic normal form (ANF), including `h_i`, has total Boolean degree **at most `D`**. An attacker receives only a forward numeric oracle for `F`, a target output `y`, the bit width, and the degree promise. The attacker does **not** receive the hidden matrices, constants, permutation coefficients, coordinate ordering, source code, or inverse.

**Proposition (mathematical derivation, awaiting external peer check).** Under this promise the oracle-only algorithm returns the *unique* `x` with `F(x)=y` after at most

`Q(n,D) = Σ_{d=1}^{n} Σ_{j=0}^{min(D,d)} binom(d,j) = Σ_{j=0}^{D} binom(n+1,j+1) - 1`

distinct forward oracle queries, without reconstructing either affine wrapper or `P`. For `n=32,D=2`, `Q=6,016`; for `n=32,D=3`, `Q=46,936`. Complexity is `O(n^(D+1))` only when `D` is fixed. In particular, this is **not** a polynomial-query result when `D` grows with `n`.

## Proof sketch and checkable invariants

1. **A genuine affine output flag always exists on a nontrivial input affine coset.** Suppose the current search domain is `S = b + span(v_1,...,v_d)`, `d>0`. After the hidden `A_in`, choose the *lowest-coordinate index `i` that varies along any direction in the coset*. All hidden coordinates below `i` are constant on the coset. Hence `h_i` is constant there and `P_i` equals a **nonconstant affine scalar function** on `S`. Because the hidden `A_out` is invertible affine, the inverse of its linear part supplies a nonzero output mask `w` such that `w·F(x)` is nonconstant and affine on `S`. This existence proof does not rely on small `D`.
2. **Bounded degree makes the affine flag provable by finitely many queries.** Parameterize `S` by `u∈GF(2)^d`. Because affine substitutions preserve the ANF-degree upper bound, each bit of `F(b+Σu_jv_j)` has degree `≤D`. For each subset `U⊆{1,...,d}` of cardinality `≤D`, evaluate the oracle on the corresponding basis sum and recover the vector ANF coefficient `c_U = XOR_{T⊆U} F(b+Σ_{j∈T}v_j)` via Boolean Möbius inversion. The output mask `w` is affine on **all** of `S` if and only if `w·c_U=0` for every `2≤|U|≤D`. These are exact GF(2) linear equations, *not* a sampled derivative heuristic. Search additionally for a direction with `w·(F(b+v_j)⊕F(b))=1`. The existence lemma guarantees consistency.
3. **Cut without losing the unknown target preimage.** Since `w·F` is genuinely affine on `S`, the equality `w·F(x)=w·y` cuts `S` into a subcoset of dimension `d−1`. The unique preimage `F^{-1}(y)` remains in that subcoset. Updating the affine basis implements the cut exactly. Iterate `n` times until the domain has one element. Independently compare its oracle output to `y` before reporting success.
4. **Query count.** At dimension `d`, at most `Σ_{j=0}^{min(D,d)} binom(d,j)` distinct inputs are necessary for the degree-`D` ANF certificate. Shared cache entries only reduce the number, and the final one-point check is already evaluated among the preceding dimension-one pair. Sum over `d=n,...,1` to obtain `Q(n,D)`.

## Stronger external-query result by composing an existing generic ANF learner

The previous `Q(n,D)` bound is a **valid but nonoptimal direct-online method**: it queries new low-weight points in each successive affine coset. This repository **already** implements exact degree-bounded full-function interpolation in [`sims/bounded-degree-oracle-functional-clone.js`](../sims/bounded-degree-oracle-functional-clone.js). Training once at the original standard basis requires only

`M(n,D)=Σ_{j=0}^{D} binom(n,j)`

forward-oracle queries. That recovers **all** vector ANF coefficients of `F` and provides a local, fully offline functionally equivalent clone under the degree promise. Applying the new affine-coset reduction to **that cloned model** needs no additional original-oracle calls. This is implemented in `trainBoundedDegreeInverse` in the new attack module. The online training bound is therefore **529 queries** for n=32,D=2 and **5,489 queries** for n=32,D=3. A defensive caller can optionally check the resulting candidate against the original oracle using **one additional** query, for 530 or 5,490 total respectively. A retrieved candidate without that check remains *conditioned on the promise*, and a false promise can yield a wrong candidate.

This 529-query full-function interpolation was already independently established in the project for ordinary dense quadratic SCO functions; **do not attribute a new source-reconstruction or functional-cloning discovery to this report**. The added mathematical argument is that for the narrower affine-wrapped triangular-permutation family the degree-bounded *forward* model can also be inverted deterministically in polynomial local work rather than requiring exponential general-purpose preimage search. A generic quadratic function need not be a permutation, and not every quadratic permutation has this hidden triangular-affine structure. The adapter's `trainingQueries` counts only real original-oracle evaluations; its `localEvaluations` counts evaluations of the learned model. A source-independent adversary can use the adapter without access to a private module once training has ended.

## Coefficient-only quadratic polar transport: one local model evaluation per preimage

The original offline method re-evaluates a learned quadratic model at many affine-coset points in order to compute restricted ANF coefficients. The independent optimization [`sims/quadratic-affine-triangular-inverse.js`](../sims/quadratic-affine-triangular-inverse.js) **avoids those repeated evaluations**. For a quadratic vector map, its discrete polar form `B(u,v)=F(x⊕u⊕v)⊕F(x⊕u)⊕F(x⊕v)⊕F(x)` is bilinear in `u,v` and independent of the base point `x`. The learner already supplies every `B(e_i,e_j)` as a degree-two ANF coefficient. The attacker maintains, on each affine coset `b+span(v_0,...,v_{d-1})`, the `d` directional deltas `delta_j=F(b⊕v_j)⊕F(b)`, all pair polar vectors `B_{jk}=B(v_j,v_k)`, and `F(b)`. All of these are transported algebraically to the next coset without a new clone evaluation.

Given an output mask `w` annihilating all `B_{jk}`, but with `a_j=w·delta_j` nonzero for some `j`, choose `p` with `a_p=1` and `c=w·(target⊕F(b))`. The half-space update is

`b' = b ⊕ c v_p`; `v'_j = v_j ⊕ a_j v_p` for `j≠p`; `F(b')=F(b)⊕c delta_p`.

The degree-two recurrences are exact:

`delta'_j = delta_j ⊕ a_j delta_p ⊕ (a_j⊕c)B_{jp}`;

`B'_{jk}=B_{jk}⊕a_j B_{pk}⊕a_k B_{jp}`.

The invariant follows from bilinearity of `B` and quadratic derivative addition; induction for `d=n,...,1` reduces to a single preimage. One **local** learned-model evaluation confirms the candidate, rather than thousands. The lemma is for quadratic maps and does not imply an inversion property for generic quadratic nonpermutations; if the source violated the degree promise, a local polynomial match does not certify the original oracle match.

## Adversarial counterexample and epistemic boundary

The test includes a separate *degree-promise violation*: a triangular permutation that swaps two inputs sharing the same lower `n−1` bits, leaving all others fixed, and then applies a randomized invertible output affine wrapper. It is a genuine compatible bijection, but its rare high-bit toggle has ANF degree `n−1`. Passing the false promise `D=2` makes the bounded-degree algorithm return **null** for the selected hidden target at `n=16,24,32`. A missing high-degree coefficient cannot be ruled out using only lower-degree Möbius queries. Final equality detects this case; it does *not* transform a false global degree premise into a proof.

This is not the same as rejecting all functions that violate the promise: some higher-degree functions may still be inverted by chance, and some may make the solver refuse earlier. The local randomized sampled-flag attack also succeeded on many dense instances and failed a deliberately sparse compatible gate. Neither result establishes a general two-sided compatible-permutation inversion theorem.

## Relevance to the primary obfuscation objective

This is **attacker-side** evidence about an explicitly promised algebraic family. It does not show anti-deobfuscation protection, 2.5× protected-code performance compliance, code integrity, unauthorized environment admission, SNO owner resistance, or SCO authentication bypass. The same chosen-query attack applies to an ordinary equal-trust minified implementation of the **same** `F` whenever the forward oracle is accessible. Under SCO, repeated *authorized* queries may expose such a functional inverse without revealing server-private source, but the claim is conditional on the function grammar and permitted query access. It should not change the repository's **RELEASE-BLOCKED** decision.
