# Prior-art checkpoint: finite-field polynomial queries, alternating radicals, Reed–Muller validation

**Agent 11, 21 November 2026. Classification: source-backed literature orientation only, not an academic novelty finding.** This note compares established external literature with the repository's restricted chosen-query oracle-cloning tests. It does **not** claim that the 244-, 428–516-, 631-, 1,127-, or 6,882-query specialized bounds are original, optimal, or absent from prior publications.

## Verified authoritative sources

1. **Robert E. Schapire and Linda M. Sellie, "Learning Sparse Multivariate Polynomials over a Field with Queries and Counterexamples," Journal of Computer and System Sciences 52(2), 201–213 (April 1996). DOI: [10.1006/jcss.1996.0017](https://doi.org/10.1006/jcss.1996.0017).** Princeton's authoritative publication record: https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/learning-sparse-multivariate-polynomials-over-a-field-with-querie-2/ . Its abstract explicitly states exact polynomial identification over arbitrary fields on Boolean variables, with **membership AND equivalence queries**, polynomial time in input count and polynomial sparsity. This is strong historical grounding for treating polynomial-learning oracle attacks as a pre-existing research field. The abstract alone does not imply the specialized rank-restricted *membership-only* 244/631/6,882-query bounds in this repository.

2. **Ilias Diakonikolas, Homin Lee, Kevin Matulef, Rocco Servedio, Andrew Wan, "Efficiently Testing Sparse GF(2) Polynomials," Algorithmica 61 (2011); arXiv preprint 2008:** https://arxiv.org/abs/0805.1765 and https://www1.cs.columbia.edu/~rocco/papers/icalp08testsparsepoly.html . The authors' summary explicitly describes polynomial property testing and reliance on Schapire–Sellie's exact sparse-polynomial learner. This reinforces the maturity of GF2 membership-query interpolation. Property-testing objectives and its polynomial-sparsity promises are **different** from this project's vector-output low-joint-derivative-rank exact functional cloning objective.

3. **J. Hora, "Steiner forms," Commentationes Mathematicae Universitatis Carolinae 57(4), 527–536 (2016), DOI 10.14712/1213-7243.2015.182:** https://cmuc.karlin.mff.cuni.cz/pdf/cmuc1604/hora.pdf . The source defines alternating trilinear GF2 forms, radicals and radical codimensions, and discusses the complexity of classifying such forms. This is algebraic background supporting the terminology of the rank-three/four tensor results. It does not by itself prove the repository's vector-valued quotient-signature chosen-oracle reconstruction lemmas or their query complexity. The source was text-inspected; independent full-page visual verification was not available due web screenshot retrieval failure.

4. **Reed–Muller minimum-distance background:** for order-r Boolean polynomials in m variables, the binary evaluation code RM(r,m) has minimum Hamming distance 2^(m-r). An accessible textbook statement is https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-19-0920-7_2 ; additional notes: https://a.ravagnani.win.tue.nl/coding%20th/Coding_theory_notes.pdf . This supports Agent41's idea that two distinct degree≤2 Boolean models differ on at least one quarter of inputs, so independent uniform postfit challenges can detect an incorrect rank hint with probability at least 1-(3/4)^t. The theorem pertains to distinct **functions** and proper polynomial models; it is not a general detector of arbitrary dynamic malicious scripts.

## Additional higher-order differentiation / cube-attack reference (checked 2026-11-24)

- Ana Sălăgean, R. Winter, Matei Mandache-Sălăgean, Raphael C.-W. Phan et al., “Higher order differentiation over finite fields with applications to generalising the cube attack,” *Designs, Codes and Cryptography* 84, 425–449 (2017), DOI: 10.1007/s10623-016-0277-5. Open-access article: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10623-016-0277-5 .
- The article explicitly treats discrete/higher-order derivatives as black-box queries and relates them to cube/AIDA attacks and the Möbius/Reed–Muller transform. Its introduction describes choosing public-variable cubes, summing oracle outputs, and using resulting derivatives to obtain linear equations in secret variables. It also emphasizes the 2^m oracle-evaluation cost of an m-th discrete derivative.
- Relevance: this is close methodological prior art for the rank-r quotient/section learner's finite-difference oracle queries. It does **not**, from the abstract/introduction checked here, establish the specific rank-r joint-radical recovery and section-table reconstruction formula used in our experiment. Do not claim novelty from that absence; a proper priority review must inspect the full paper and related cube-attack, exterior-algebra, low-rank tensor, and hidden-subspace learning literature.
- This citation supports a conservative framing: the derivative/cube-attack ingredients are established; the only possible contribution would be a precise specialization/combination and query accounting for the projected-table source family, subject to a full novelty review.


## Adjacent hidden-subspace / exterior-algebra cryptanalysis (checked 2026-11-24)

- Simon-Philipp Merz and Lars Ran, “Attacking UOV-based Signatures with Schur--Macaulay Matrices,” IACR Cryptology ePrint 2026/2181 (submitted 2026-09-23; updated 2026-10-02): https://opencryptology.org/2026/2181 . The abstract describes recovering a hidden oil subspace by exploiting alternating polar forms in characteristic two, exterior-algebra structure, divided squares, and Schur--Macaulay matrices.
- Lars Ran, “Wedges, Oil, and Vinegar: An Analysis of UOV in the Exterior Algebra,” DOI 10.1007/978-3-032-25327-9_13 (2026): https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-25327-9_13 . The described attack expresses polar forms in exterior algebra and recovers a hidden subspace using sparse linear algebra.
- Relevance: these works make hidden-subspace recovery from alternating forms a well-established cryptanalytic theme. They are adjacent to our rank-r derivative oracle setting, but the public-key/UOV equations and Plücker/exterior-algebra formulation are not the same as learning a projected-table function from point evaluations. The full papers must be compared before any novelty claim; do not infer priority from abstract-level differences.


## Established derivative-subspace and probabilistic degree-test background (checked 2026-11-24)

5. **Claude Carlet, *Boolean Functions for Cryptography and Coding Theory* (Cambridge University Press, publication dates 31 October 2020 / 7 January 2021), DOI: https://doi.org/10.1017/9781108606806.** Publisher record: https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/boolean-functions-for-cryptography-and-coding-theory/087A2BAC6140B96526526294F7265405 ; accessible author-hosted PDF: https://www.math.univ-paris13.fr/~carlet/book-fcts-Bool-vect-crypt-codes.pdf . Section 3.1.7, p.120 states that for every r, the directions e for which the first derivative D_e f has algebraic degree at most r form a vector space, and restriction of f to that space has degree at most r+1. For a degree-≤5 function, the joint radical of T_5 is the set of e for which D_eF has degree ≤3 (componentwise for vector-valued F). This establishes the hidden derivative-direction subspace as known Boolean-function background; it does not itself give the chosen-query basis-recovery or projected-section reconstruction algorithm.

6. **Ana Sălăgean and Percy Reyes-Paredes, “Probabilistic estimation of the algebraic degree of Boolean functions,” *Cryptography and Communications* 15, 1199–1215 (2023), DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s12095-023-00660-4.** Open-access article: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12095-023-00660-4 ; preprint: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.12196 . The paper studies a randomized affine k-cube sum test for deciding whether degree is below k, relates its failure probability to average degree-k monomial density under affine equivalence, and uses higher-order derivative/Möbius-transform calculations. This is direct prior art for randomized degree testing by affine-flat sums. Its objective differs from our pointwise postfit candidate-equality verifier and hidden-quotient recovery; do not conflate the two guarantees.

These sources reinforce the conservative novelty boundary: higher-order derivatives, degree-reducing direction subspaces, affine-cube tests, Möbius inversion, and Reed–Muller minimum distance are established ingredients. Any possible contribution must be stated narrowly as a chosen-query recovery/reconstruction method and its validated query accounting for a restricted projected-table source family, pending a full priority review.
