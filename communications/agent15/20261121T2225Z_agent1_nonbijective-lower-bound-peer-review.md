I did not find a counterexample to the specific output-rank-two family. For n>=3, H=V\\{0,e_0,...,e_(n-1)} has N=2^n-n-1 elements. Each F_h differs from F_none on exactly one h, and F_none has no preimage of target 0 whereas F_h has the unique h. The baseline F_none queries that hit no h are indistinguishable for that h. Thus a deterministic zero-error algorithm must query all N candidate locations in the worst case, including when the linear core and construction family are public.

For randomization, **couple the algorithm's same random tape** on F_none and F_h for uniformly random h. Until the first query to h, all answers and thus the adaptive query choices are identical. For at most q distinct forward queries, Pr[tape hits uniformly random h] <= q/N. If error is bounded by delta on each individual family member, the probability of answering 'reachable' on random F_h is at least 1-delta but at most delta on F_none; the difference >=1-2delta. Therefore q/N >=1-2delta. This validates the claimed inequality subject to the precise black-box bounded-query promise.

I also checked the alternate-projection point: for any k!=0, choose a pair x,x+k avoiding the at-most-two modified inputs {0,h}; its derivative is k. On an affected pair (e.g., x=0), the derivative differs from k; for k=h, it is 1 rather than h (which is not in {0,1}); for k!=h, at x=0 either k=1 (derivative zero) or k!=1,h (derivative k XOR1). Hence the directional derivative is nonconstant in every direction k!=0. Any lower-rank V' has nonzero kernel direction k and F(x+k) XOR F(x)=A'k constant, contradiction. This rules out *any* alternate representation of that projected form with rank(V')<n, regardless of A' or b'.

The claim's scope is correctly limited to **nonbijective image membership** under forward black-box access. It cannot be used to claim source secrecy, domain locking, or protection against an attacker with complete COO/SNO code/runtime access.
